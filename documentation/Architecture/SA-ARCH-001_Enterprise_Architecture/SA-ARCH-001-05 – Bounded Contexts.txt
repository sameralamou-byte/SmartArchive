1. Executive Summary
The Bounded Contexts document defines the logical boundaries that organize the SmartArchive Enterprise Platform into independent, cohesive, and business-oriented domains. It establishes a clear separation of responsibilities between functional areas while ensuring consistent communication through well-defined integration contracts, domain events, and shared enterprise standards.
As SmartArchive evolves into a large-scale enterprise platform supporting intelligent document management, artificial intelligence, workflow automation, analytics, security, compliance, and marketplace services, maintaining a single monolithic business model becomes increasingly difficult. Different business capabilities often use the same terminology with different meanings, resulting in ambiguity, duplicated functionality, and unnecessary architectural complexity.
This document addresses these challenges by applying the principles of Domain-Driven Design (DDD) and organizing the enterprise into multiple Bounded Contexts. Each bounded context owns its own business model, business rules, data, services, and lifecycle while exposing controlled interfaces for collaboration with other contexts. This approach enables every business capability to evolve independently without negatively affecting other parts of the platform.
The SmartArchive architecture defines dedicated bounded contexts for Identity Management, Document Management, Artificial Intelligence, Search & Discovery, Workflow Automation, Security, Compliance & Governance, Integration Services, Analytics & Reporting, Marketplace Services, Platform Services, and Future Capability domains. Together, these contexts provide a scalable architectural foundation capable of supporting future enterprise growth while preserving maintainability, security, and operational stability.
This document also describes the relationships between bounded contexts, including upstream and downstream dependencies, shared kernel components, anti-corruption layers, integration contracts, domain events, and ownership boundaries. These architectural mechanisms ensure that information flows efficiently across the enterprise while preventing tight coupling between independently evolving domains.
The bounded context architecture supports SmartArchive's strategic objectives of cloud-native scalability, modular deployment, AI-driven document intelligence, enterprise interoperability, and continuous product evolution. It enables individual development teams to work independently within clearly defined ownership boundaries while maintaining consistency across the overall enterprise architecture.
This document should be used by Enterprise Architects, Solution Architects, Software Architects, Technical Leads, Development Teams, AI Engineers, Integration Engineers, Security Architects, DevOps Engineers, Product Owners, and Governance Teams responsible for designing, implementing, maintaining, and evolving the SmartArchive Enterprise Platform.
The Bounded Contexts document complements the SmartArchive Enterprise Architecture Repository and should be read together with the Enterprise Architecture Vision, Architecture Principles, Business Capability Model, Domain Model, Component Architecture, Integration Architecture, Security Architecture, and Solution Architecture documents. Collectively, these documents establish the architectural foundation for delivering a scalable, secure, maintainable, and enterprise-ready SmartArchive platform.
2. Purpose
The purpose of this document is to define the Bounded Context Architecture for the SmartArchive Enterprise Platform using the principles of Domain-Driven Design (DDD). It establishes clear business boundaries that separate the platform into independent domains, each responsible for its own business logic, terminology, data ownership, and lifecycle.
This document provides the architectural foundation for organizing SmartArchive into cohesive business contexts that can evolve independently while maintaining controlled communication through standardized interfaces, domain events, APIs, and integration contracts.
The Bounded Context architecture enables SmartArchive to reduce business complexity by preventing unrelated domains from sharing conflicting business models or responsibilities. Each context encapsulates its own business rules and exposes only the capabilities required by other contexts, improving maintainability, scalability, security, and long-term architectural stability.
This document also supports enterprise governance by clearly identifying ownership responsibilities, integration boundaries, data authority, upstream and downstream relationships, and collaboration mechanisms between business domains. These definitions reduce coupling between components and enable multiple development teams to work in parallel without introducing architectural conflicts.
The SmartArchive Enterprise Platform relies on bounded contexts to support advanced capabilities including intelligent document processing, artificial intelligence services, workflow automation, enterprise integrations, compliance management, analytics, and future platform expansion. By isolating these capabilities into well-defined business contexts, the platform can continuously evolve while minimizing the impact of future architectural changes.
The purpose of this document is also to serve as a reference for Enterprise Architects, Solution Architects, Software Architects, Developers, AI Engineers, Integration Engineers, Security Teams, DevOps Engineers, Product Owners, and Governance Teams responsible for designing, implementing, integrating, and maintaining SmartArchive business services.
This document ensures that all future architecture, application development, microservices, APIs, data models, and deployment decisions remain aligned with the enterprise business boundaries established for the SmartArchive Enterprise Platform.

4. Bounded Context Overview
4.1 Overview
The SmartArchive Enterprise Platform is organized into a collection of independent Bounded Contexts, each representing a distinct business domain with clearly defined responsibilities, ownership, business rules, data models, and service boundaries.
Following the principles of Domain-Driven Design (DDD), each bounded context encapsulates its own business language, entities, services, workflows, and integration mechanisms while minimizing dependencies on other contexts. This separation enables the platform to evolve incrementally without introducing unnecessary complexity or affecting unrelated business capabilities.
The Bounded Context Architecture serves as the organizational framework that aligns business capabilities with technical implementation. It provides a structured approach for designing modular services, defining ownership boundaries, and supporting independent deployment, scalability, governance, and long-term maintainability.
Within SmartArchive, bounded contexts are designed to communicate through well-defined APIs, domain events, messaging, and standardized integration contracts rather than through direct access to internal business logic or data stores. This architectural approach reduces coupling, improves resilience, and enables parallel development across multiple engineering teams.
The bounded context architecture also establishes the foundation for future microservices, modular deployment strategies, AI service orchestration, enterprise integrations, cloud-native scalability, and continuous platform evolution.

4.2 Objectives
The SmartArchive Bounded Context Architecture has the following objectives:
• Define clear ownership for each business capability. 
• Eliminate ambiguity in business terminology. 
• Separate independent business models. 
• Reduce coupling between domains. 
• Support independent development teams. 
• Improve maintainability and scalability. 
• Enable modular deployment and future microservices. 
• Support enterprise governance. 
• Simplify AI and integration architecture. 
• Provide a stable foundation for future platform growth.

5. Domain-Driven Design (DDD) Principles
5.1 Overview
The SmartArchive Enterprise Platform adopts Domain-Driven Design (DDD) as the primary architectural approach for modeling complex business processes, defining clear ownership boundaries, and organizing enterprise capabilities into cohesive and independently evolving business contexts.
DDD provides a structured methodology for aligning software architecture with business objectives by ensuring that technical solutions accurately represent real-world business concepts, processes, and terminology. Within SmartArchive, DDD enables the platform to remain scalable, maintainable, and adaptable as new capabilities, AI services, integrations, and enterprise requirements are introduced.
The Bounded Context Architecture defined in this document is a direct application of DDD Strategic Design principles and serves as the foundation for modular application development, enterprise governance, and future microservices.

5.2 Business-Centric Design
All architectural decisions shall begin with the business domain rather than the technical implementation.
Business capabilities, processes, terminology, and enterprise objectives shall determine how bounded contexts are defined, ensuring that software components accurately reflect business responsibilities.
Each bounded context shall represent a complete business capability with clearly defined ownership and accountability.

5.3 Ubiquitous Language
Each bounded context shall establish and maintain a Ubiquitous Language shared by business stakeholders, architects, developers, AI engineers, and product owners.
Business terminology shall be:
• Consistent. 
• Unambiguous. 
• Well documented. 
• Shared across all project documentation. 
• Used consistently in APIs, data models, source code, and architecture diagrams. 
Terms may differ between bounded contexts where business meaning requires different interpretations.

5.4 Bounded Context Isolation
Each bounded context shall operate independently and maintain ownership of:
• Business rules. 
• Business processes. 
• Entities. 
• Aggregate Roots. 
• Value Objects. 
• Domain Services. 
• Domain Events. 
• Data storage. 
• APIs. 
• Integration contracts. 
No context shall directly modify the internal implementation or persistence layer of another context.

5.5 High Cohesion
Each bounded context shall contain business functionality that is closely related and focused on a single business responsibility.
Business logic that belongs together shall remain within the same context to maximize maintainability, readability, and long-term evolution.

5.6 Loose Coupling
Communication between bounded contexts shall occur only through approved integration mechanisms, including:
• Versioned APIs. 
• Domain Events. 
• Message Queues. 
• Integration Services. 
• Shared Kernel components (where explicitly approved). 
Direct dependencies between internal implementations shall be avoided.

5.7 Aggregate Consistency
Aggregate boundaries shall enforce business consistency.
Each Aggregate shall:
• Have one Aggregate Root. 
• Protect business invariants. 
• Control access to child entities. 
• Maintain transactional consistency within its boundary. 
• Avoid unnecessary cross-context transactions. 

5.8 Context Ownership
Every bounded context shall have clearly defined ownership, including:
• Business Owner. 
• Product Owner. 
• Architecture Owner. 
• Technical Owner. 
Ownership includes responsibility for:
• Business rules. 
• Data integrity. 
• APIs. 
• Events. 
• Documentation. 
• Lifecycle management. 
• Continuous improvement. 

5.9 Independent Evolution
Bounded contexts shall evolve independently whenever practical.
Changes within one context should have minimal impact on other contexts, enabling:
• Parallel development. 
• Independent deployment. 
• Faster innovation. 
• Reduced technical debt. 
• Better scalability. 

5.10 Integration by Contract
Interactions between contexts shall be governed by explicit contracts.
Contracts may include:
• REST APIs. 
• Event schemas. 
• Message formats. 
• Integration interfaces. 
• Shared service agreements. 
Changes to integration contracts shall follow versioning and governance procedures.

5.11 Continuous Architecture Governance
The SmartArchive Enterprise Architecture Governance Board shall review significant changes to:
• Context boundaries. 
• Domain ownership. 
• Shared Kernels. 
• Integration contracts. 
• Aggregate definitions. 
• Context relationships. 
Any deviation from these principles shall require an approved Architecture Decision Record (ADR) before implementation.

9. Shared Kernel
9.1 Overview
The Shared Kernel defines the limited set of business assets that may be shared between bounded contexts within the SmartArchive Enterprise Platform. It provides a controlled mechanism for reusing common business concepts while preserving the independence, ownership, and autonomy of each bounded context.
The SmartArchive Enterprise Platform follows the Domain-Driven Design (DDD) principle that sharing should be minimized. Only business assets that are stable, widely used, and jointly governed should be included in the Shared Kernel. All other business logic, entities, and services shall remain within their respective bounded contexts.
The Shared Kernel reduces duplication, promotes consistency, and improves interoperability while avoiding unnecessary coupling between business domains.

9.2 Objectives
The Shared Kernel has the following objectives:
• Promote consistent business terminology. 
• Reduce duplication of common assets. 
• Standardize reusable enterprise components. 
• Improve interoperability between bounded contexts. 
• Support enterprise governance. 
• Preserve context independence. 
• Simplify integration across business domains. 
• Enable controlled reuse of shared business concepts.

10. Anti-Corruption Layers (ACL)
10.1 Overview
An Anti-Corruption Layer (ACL) is a protective architectural boundary that prevents external systems, legacy applications, third-party services, or neighboring bounded contexts from introducing incompatible business models into the SmartArchive Enterprise Platform.
The SmartArchive Enterprise Platform adopts the Anti-Corruption Layer pattern defined by Domain-Driven Design (DDD) to preserve the integrity of each bounded context while enabling controlled interoperability with external and internal systems.
Rather than allowing direct interaction with foreign business models, every integration requiring business model translation shall pass through an Anti-Corruption Layer. This layer is responsible for translating data structures, business terminology, message formats, and service contracts into the language and model of the owning bounded context.
The use of ACLs ensures that SmartArchive remains modular, maintainable, and resilient while supporting long-term platform evolution and enterprise integration.

10.2 Objectives
The Anti-Corruption Layer architecture has the following objectives:
• Protect bounded contexts from foreign business models. 
• Prevent propagation of legacy design decisions. 
• Isolate external system dependencies. 
• Translate business terminology between domains. 
• Preserve internal domain integrity. 
• Support independent evolution of bounded contexts. 
• Simplify enterprise integration. 
• Reduce architectural coupling.
